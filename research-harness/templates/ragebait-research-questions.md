# Pilot Research Brief: Why Ragebait Works Even When You Know It Is Ragebait

**Status:** Research question only. No conclusion is pre-approved.  
**Format target:** 10-minute video essay  
**Working audience:** English-speaking adults interested in psychology and internet culture  
**Narration style:** Creator speaking conversationally from verified notes, not reading a generic essay.

## Central question

Why do people sometimes keep engaging with provocative online content even when they recognize that the content may be deliberately inflammatory or manipulative?

Avoid assuming that one mechanism explains every case. "Ragebait" is an internet label, not necessarily a validated scientific category with one agreed definition.

## Questions to investigate

1. **Definition:** How do platforms, researchers, journalists, and users use the term "ragebait"? Which distinctions matter between outrage, moral anger, trolling, clickbait, and engagement farming?
2. **Emotion and attention:** What evidence shows that emotionally charged or morally provocative content changes sharing, commenting, or attention? Does the evidence measure anger specifically, or emotion in general?
3. **Knowing versus doing:** What research explains the gap between recognizing persuasive tactics and still responding to them? Distinguish awareness, impulse, habit, identity, social pressure, and deliberate participation.
4. **Platform incentives:** What is known about ranking/recommendation systems and engagement signals? Do not claim that a platform boosts anger specifically unless the source actually tests that claim.
5. **Social reward:** How do audience, status, belonging, moral signalling, quote-posting, and correction attempts affect engagement? Which claims are supported by research versus interpretation?
6. **Backfire and limits:** When does outrage reduce engagement, trigger unfollows, create fatigue, or damage trust? Seek evidence against a universal "anger always wins" story.
7. **Individual differences:** Do findings vary by platform, topic, culture, age, political identity, or type of user? Avoid turning a sample from one setting into a claim about everyone.
8. **Creator incentives:** What can be established about incentives to post provocative content? Separate documented platform mechanics, creator testimony, and speculation about motives.
9. **Counterexamples:** Find at least two credible studies or cases that complicate the working narrative.
10. **Practical takeaway:** What, if anything, does evidence suggest people can do to interrupt unwanted engagement without implying that all anger or moral criticism is irrational?

## Research lanes

- **Lane A: Primary research and reviews.** Peer-reviewed studies, meta-analyses, datasets, methods and limitations.
- **Lane B: Platform and industry evidence.** Official platform documentation, transparency reports, research releases and documented changes. Treat corporate explanations as interested sources, not neutral proof.
- **Lane C: Books and conceptual frameworks.** Verify exact book edition, author, publisher, year, chapter/page and the specific concept used. Do not invent page numbers.

After the first pass, assign one agent to counterevidence and one to independently explain the findings before writing any script.

## Asset capture plan

Capture only artifacts with a clear use in the video or fact-checking:
- Relevant page screenshot showing the specific paragraph, table, chart, or passage.
- PDF copy plus page-specific screenshot where the PDF is legitimately accessible and the page is used.
- Book bibliographic record and verified excerpt/locator, not a fabricated page screenshot.
- Platform UI screen recording only when demonstrating a real, current interface and following applicable terms.
- Original diagrams made from verified claims, labelled as explanatory models rather than source data.
- Article snapshot for a source that may change or whose exact wording/layout matters.

For every local artifact, register its source, relative path, description, hash, capture method, rights status, and review status. A research screenshot is not automatically approved for publication.

## Script acceptance criteria

- The opening asks a real question and does not announce a conclusion before evidence.
- Every factual claim has a source locator in the claim ledger.
- Correlation is not described as causation.
- At least one strong counterargument is fairly represented.
- The creator can explain each section naturally from notes.
- A 10-minute target is achieved by pacing and substance, not padding.
- The final script includes visual cues tied to asset IDs or explicit new-asset requests.
